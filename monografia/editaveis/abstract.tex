\begin{resumo}[Abstract]
    Dengue is one of the main public health challenges in Brazil, and the 2024 epidemic, with about 6.6 million notifications, highlighted the need for tools that anticipate outbreaks. This work developed and evaluated a model that forecasts weekly dengue notifications four weeks ahead for the 137 Brazilian mesoregions, integrating SINAN microdata with data from about 700 INMET automatic weather stations through a reproducible layered pipeline. A data audit identified and corrected defects in the public files, including a shift in the column mapping of the INMET files that made the variable treated as humidity correspond to the dew point, and showed that part of the preliminary results were artifacts of these defects. Three training-window variants of XGBoost (4~years, 22~years, and 22~years with recency weighting), each with and without climate variables, were compared with naive forecasters on a 2024--2026 test set. The best model (22-year long series with enriched climate) reached $R^2_{\log}$ of 0.89, against 0.82 for persistence, and placed the predicted peak within two weeks of the observed peak in 48\% of the outbreak seasons, against 4\% for persistence, with no systematic delay; in the typical years (2025 and 2026), it outperformed persistence on every metric. In the 2024 epidemic, however, it predicted about 59\% of the notifications, and persistence was better on the original scale, which agrees with the literature reporting that the magnitude of peaks is harder to forecast than their timing. The long series outperformed the short one by $+0.015$ in $R^2_{\log}$ ($p < 0.001$), and climate variables provided a small but statistically consistent gain ($+0.008$), present in every temporal validation window and in 23 of the 27 federative units. For risk-level classification, the long series raised the macro F1 from 0.50 to 0.59. The results were made available through an interactive dashboard and public datasets.

     \noindent
     \textbf{Keywords}: Dengue. Forecasting. Machine learning. XGBoost. Climate. Epidemiological surveillance.
\end{resumo}
